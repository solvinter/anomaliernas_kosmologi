# Expansion Policy

AI does not get permission to "make things up" in the core of the book.

AI is responsible for the physical reality of the story.

That distinction is central.

## When The Author Writes

> Han berättade om Solvinter.

The workshop may decide:

- which room they sit in
- whether it is morning or evening
- how the coffee smells
- what the light does
- what the table is made of
- whether someone looks out through the window
- how long the pause is
- how the voice changes
- whether someone stands up
- what sound enters from the street

The workshop may not decide:

- that Solvinter means something else
- that Ghana is replaced by another place
- that the relationship between father and son changes
- that the philosophical point is reversed
- that a character's function is transformed without author approval

## Sacred Material

The following must be preserved unless the author explicitly changes it:

- Betelskeppet as literal and symbolic underworld
- the cranes as recurring presence
- secrets as ethical danger
- anomalies as pressure against old maps
- Cow Lane before BlackRocks in the Ghana sequence
- BlackRocks as built by the protagonist and gradually occupied by the father
- Solvinter as human-centered, aesthetic, practical and almost invisible
- technology as tool, not spectacle
- Ghana as place with existing knowledge, builders, dignity and agency
- Lelo/Eira ambiguity
- the future as everyday life, not science fiction display

## Physical World Mandate

Every rendered scene should contain enough concrete reality that the reader can stand inside it.

This may include:

- air
- light
- temperature
- sound
- smell
- furniture
- tools
- street noise
- bodily posture
- silence
- how people enter or leave the room

Physical detail is not decoration. It is the way ideas become visible.
