# Cinematic Guidelines

The goal is not merely a book.

The goal is a novel that already feels filmed.

Every scene should be imaginable as a film scene.

Readers should always know:

- where they are
- who is present
- what is physically happening
- what emotional change occurs
- why the scene exists

## Camera Without Camera Tricks

Do not write screenplay directions.

Instead, let the prose behave as if it knows:

- what the first image of the scene is
- what object or gesture anchors the scene
- when the reader should move closer
- when the room should widen
- what detail remains after the scene ends

## Scene Entry

Enter scenes as late as possible, but not so late that the reader floats without ground.

Usually establish:

- place
- body
- immediate tension

## Scene Exit

Exit on:

- a changed understanding
- an image
- a line of dialogue
- an unresolved pressure
- a physical gesture that carries meaning

Avoid ending scenes with explanation if an image can carry the same weight.

## Sensory Anchors

Every page should contain at least one anchor from the physical world:

- light
- sound
- smell
- touch
- temperature
- texture
- bodily posture
- tool or object

The anchor should serve the scene. It should not merely decorate it.
